Dense star clusters and dark-matter halos near black holes obey the Einstein-Vlasov system, where collisionless matter moves under the spacetime curvature it generates. Wu and Most introduced a numerical-relativity particle-in-cell method using large ensembles of phase-space macroparticles. We ask whether the published text suffices to rebuild the method independently and which strong-field physics survives with at most ten thousand macroparticles.

From that text alone, large-language-model agents under human direction built the code on the adaptive octree mesh, constraint-damped Einstein evolution, moving-puncture gauge, and Kokkos layer of AthenaK, checking every stage against independent analytical solutions. Each cycle interpolates the geometry on a four-point cubic Hermite stencil, advances macroparticles along geodesics via the implicit midpoint pusher or spacetime Boris pusher, and deposits cloud-in-cell sources before spacetime advances.

The two pushers reproduce published Kerr geodesic errors once the local-frame tetrad gauge of the spacetime Boris pusher is specified: the symmetric gauge matches analytical-metric benchmarks, while the Cholesky gauge matches Cartesian-mesh benchmarks. Deposition conserves total sources to rounding error across refinement boundaries, while level-capped deposition keeps clouds wider than the interparticle spacing as refinement divides macroparticles per cell by eight. Monoenergetic cluster equilibria, the dust-collapse lapse bound, and an orbital-scale correction to a published setup follow at full precision.
