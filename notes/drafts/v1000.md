# Numerical-relativity particle-in-cell method at low particle number: Tetrad gauge, level-capped deposition, and collapse

Dense star clusters, dark-matter spikes, and colliding streams near black holes evolve under collective gravity, obeying the Einstein-Vlasov system where collisionless matter follows geodesics of the curvature it generates. Wu and Most introduced a numerical-relativity particle-in-cell (NRPIC) method using large ensembles of phase-space packets called macroparticles. Rebuilding the method from its published text alone tests whether that description specifies the algorithm completely and which strong-field physics survives with at most ten thousand macroparticles.

Because geodesics are the characteristics of the Vlasov equation, advancing fixed-mass macroparticles through six phase-space variables, position and covariant spatial momentum, while sourcing Einstein's equations with their moments integrates the Einstein-Vlasov system. Spatial slices evolve in coordinate time through the spatial metric, the lapse relating proper to coordinate time, and the shift tracking coordinate motion relative to normal observers. Each cycle advances a constraint-damped conformal decomposition of spatial metric and extrinsic curvature in moving-puncture gauge with logarithmic slicing and hyperbolic shift evolution, pushes macroparticles in the time-averaged metric, applies lapse excision inside black holes, deposits sources, and adapts the mesh.

The two pushers, the implicit midpoint pusher and the spacetime Boris pusher, attain second-order accuracy via fixed-point iteration. Evaluating the characteristic equations in coordinates at the time-space midpoint fixes the midpoint update uniquely, conserves cyclic momenta to rounding error, and reproduces every published digit. The spacetime Boris pusher projects momentum onto an orthonormal triad of normal observers, casting the tetrad equation of motion into Lorentz-force form with momentum-contracted gravito-electric and gravito-magnetic fields advanced by a half-step kick, the Boris rotation, and a half-step kick. Because the spatial metric fixes the triad only up to a local rotation, this update carries a tetrad gauge freedom: triad derivatives enter the fields as frame-rotation forces while endpoint transformations rotate momentum into and out of the frame. Continuous motion cancels frame rotation against endpoint rotations, whereas discrete midpoint evaluation leaves a second-order error set by how the tetrad gauge partitions metric gradients into triad derivatives. On a near-capture Kerr passage, with Schwarzschild as the nonrotating limit, the two tetrad gauges resolve an unstated choice in Wu and Most: the symmetric gauge, the matrix square root of the spatial metric, matches published analytical spherical Kerr-Schild errors, while the Cholesky gauge, triangular orthogonalization in Cartesian order, matches published Cartesian-mesh errors under four-point cubic Hermite interpolation, where interpolation sets the error.

Each macroparticle deposits the ten source components seen by normal observers, energy density, momentum density, and symmetric spatial stress, via a piecewise-linear cloud-in-cell shape function over eight receiving cells and binomial filtering with weights one-quarter, one-half, and one-quarter across ghost-filled interfaces. Dividing cell totals by the proper spatial volume element turns unit-sum weights into exact volume integrals. Across refinement boundaries, cell-sized clouds mismatch where coarse and fine stencils meet; assigning each macroparticle a single representation level lays its cloud on a virtual uniform lattice of unit weight, summing virtual cells in coarser blocks into enclosing coarse leaf cells and splitting virtual cells over finer blocks uniformly across nested fine leaf cells. Because regridding preserves particle states, the conservation identity holds to rounding error across adaptive octree meshes and their uniform and statically refined limits.

Smooth kinetic evolution requires many overlapping clouds per cell, since sampling noise scales as the inverse square root of macroparticles per cell. Resolving strong-field gradients and apparent horizons, the outermost closed slice surfaces whose outgoing light rays converge, drives octree refinement, where halving cell edges divides cell volume and mean macroparticles per cell by eight per level. At ten thousand macroparticles, fine cells shrink below the interparticle spacing; tying cloud width to the finest local cell separates clouds into isolated spikes among empty cells, dropping central dust-collapse density below one-half of the cycloid until contraction crowds particles together. Level-capped deposition bounds each representation level using the preceding coordinate energy density to maintain a target neighbor count, acting as an adaptive smoothing length while preserving conservation and recovering the published uncapped limit.
