# 2026-11-13

## Decisions
- Geodesic pushers live in one header that sees the spacetime only through a metric
  functor; the same kernels run standalone, with analytic metrics, on a virtual lattice,
  and on mesh data.
- Tetrad gauge of the spacetime Boris pusher is selectable. The published analytic
  (spherical Kerr-Schild) invariant errors are reproduced by the symmetric square-root
  gauge; the published Cartesian-grid errors by the lower Cholesky gauge in (x, y, z)
  order. Neither gauge reproduces both sets.
- Ownership and deposition use a search over MeshBlocks (single rank). Deposition is
  conservative on uniform, SMR and AMR meshes by construction.
- With at most 10^4 particles the published AMR deposition (cloud at the finest local
  level) makes the source granular once the mesh is finer than the particle spacing.
  Added a cap on the representation level (`deposit_max_level`); with the cap inactive
  the scheme is the published one.
- AthenaK requires an even number of ghost zones with mesh refinement, so refined runs
  use nghost = 4 (sixth-order differences) or 2 (second-order); the original quotes
  fourth order.

## Later the same day

### Decisions
- Density-adaptive cloud level: the cap of each particle follows the coordinate density
  of the previous deposit (target particles per cloud cell), bounded by a floor level.
- Collapse and cluster meshes use 16^3 MeshBlocks: a nested octree around one centre has
  the same number of blocks per level regardless of block size, so 16^3 blocks cost one
  eighth of 32^3 blocks at equal resolution in the matter region.
- Nested regions must end inside the next coarser block layer, otherwise the two-to-one
  rule cascades refinement outward (seen as 2136 instead of 1128 blocks).
- Horizon finder: tolerance 1e-5, search every 16 cycles (see docs/TESTLOG.md).
- Cluster with two overdensities: the envelope is taken with areal radius 13.3 M
  (y_c = 0.907), for which the quoted clump speed 0.133215 is the circular speed to
  2e-7; the stated 30 M gives 0.103 and is consistent with the diameter in the
  published figure.

- History gained the proper-volume integrals of the sources after the filter.

## 05:00 to 06:25: three things that turned out differently than assumed

1. **Net momentum of a sample.** A sample of 10^4 particles has a mean velocity of
   about 1e-3; the cluster drifts by 0.2 M in 300 M. Removed in the generator. Checked
   by a run that differs only in this (drift gone).

2. **The stable cluster's breathing is not sampling noise.** First reading: noise of
   the sample. Test: three seeds. Result: identical outward excursion (15 percent in
   radius, 35 percent in central density) for all three, so the reading was wrong.
   Second reading: the binomial filter widens every cloud, the source becomes softer
   than the equilibrium the velocities were drawn for. Tests: excursion against number
   of passes (0, 2, 4), then a compensated filter that keeps the passes and cancels the
   widening. Both came out as predicted within a factor of about 1.5. The plain filter
   also lowers the total deposited energy by 0.3 percent, which was not anticipated.
   Without any filter the cluster does not expand but wanders irregularly and the
   constraint norms are ten times larger; cause of that not determined.
   -> cluster runs use four passes plus compensation.

3. **The horizon mass was wrong, and not for the reason first given.** First reading
   of the 0.974 after dust collapse: the finder stops too early. Test on a vacuum
   puncture, where the answer is known: tight tolerance changes nothing (3e-5). The
   slice's own mass (Misner-Sharp, from the restart dumps) is right to 0.15 percent at
   M/8 while the finder is 2.3 percent low, and 5.9 percent low at M/16. Cause found in
   the vendored finder: it interpolates a derivative array whose ghost cells are never
   filled. With derivatives taken from the metric interpolant the finder agrees with
   the slice mass to 3e-4 on the same data. All earlier horizon masses are withdrawn.

What this changes for the paper: the low-particle-number story has a second ingredient
besides the level cap (filter compensation), and the horizon masses must come from the
corrected finder, cross-checked by the Misner-Sharp mass in the spherical runs. The
defect in the horizon finder belongs to the host code as vendored (commit c5a0d7f) and
is worth reporting upstream; that is for the user to decide.

Method note for myself: in both cases the first explanation I wrote down was a guess
that fitted, and I logged it as the cause. Each was settled only by a run in which the
guess could fail. From here on an entry names a cause only after such a run, and says
"not determined" otherwise.
