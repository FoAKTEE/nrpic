\section{Benchmark metrics for colliding beams and Kerr tidal disruption}

Table~\ref{tab:supp_sr} compiles full quantitative convergence, energy conservation, normalized profile error, and runtime ratios for the three-dimensional flat-spacetime tests across both the continuum moving-finite-volume (MFM) solver~\cite{Hopkins:2026lbv} and the hybrid symplectic-cubature / asymptotic-preserving (SCP-AP) branch~\cite{Stroud:1971,Wu:2026nrpic}. All normalized compute times $T_{\rm comp}/T_{15}$ divide by the $2400$-element $\sigma/\mu_p = 15$ continuum time. For collisionless colliding beams ($\sigma/\mu_p = 0$), automatic expansion into $33600$ deterministic cubature packets eliminates counter-streaming density oscillations, holds total normal-observer energy invariant to machine precision, and reduces relative $L_1$ error against the $512$-node exact characteristic solution by nearly fivefold compared to purely continuum MFM while executing at lower compute cost.

\section{Dynamical collapse, strong scaling, and device profiling}

Table~\ref{tab:supp_os_scale} summarizes the Oppenheimer--Snyder dynamical collapse test ($R_0 = 5\,M$, seven-level adaptive grid, $t = 0$ to $25\,M$) alongside strong scaling over fifty fixed Kerr integration cycles up to $N_{\rm eff} = 200592$. Device trace profiling confirms that replacing nested Brent iterations with safeguarded analytical Newton solves, caching basis pair indices outside inner gradient loops, batching local neighbor gather counts into single atomic updates, and eliminating large device view captures moved every continuum flux, gradient, relaxation, and geometry kernel out of device constant-memory parameter staging into local launch paths without altering physical parity ($|\Delta \bm{x}|, |\Delta m|, |\Delta \bar{\bm{u}}|, |\Delta \Sigma| \le 2.0 \times 10^{-15}$ on unit verification tests).
