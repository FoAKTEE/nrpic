\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We have formulated, implemented, and benchmarked a hybrid symplectic-cubature and asymptotic-preserving moving-finite-volume solver for the three-plus-one general-relativistic Boltzmann-Vlasov system inside AthenaK. Splitting the transport operator along physical collisionality bridges two complementary regimes inside one unified initial-data and field-coupling architecture.

For warm or shock-forming collisional matter ($\sigma / \mu_p > 0$), spatial smoothing-length hash construction, compact plain-old-data kernel structures, fast Sylvester determinant positivity checks, combined Chebyshev-asymptotic Bessel evaluations, and safeguarded analytical Newton face-speed and temperature inversions eliminate memory spills and convergence bottlenecks. Combined with exact Valencia Synge conservative-to-primitive recovery, symmetric contact Riemann waves, and harmonic sound-speed thermal conduction, the asymptotic-preserving moment solver transitions continuously across four decades of optical depth to recover the relativistic Taub shock jump within roughly one percent while improving curved-space compute efficiency by an order of magnitude without sacrificing strict conservation.

For collisionless flows ($\sigma / \mu_p \le 0$), one-time fifth-degree Stroud cubature expansion with base-$2,3,5$ Halton-$SO(3)$ frame rotation transforms continuous velocity-basis moments into deterministic characteristic packets evolved by implicit midpoint geodesic integration. On special-relativistic colliding beams, Kerr ($a = 0.9M$) tidal disruption, and full-relativity Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse with Z4c adaptive mesh refinement and FastFlow horizon tracking, the hybrid cubature branch removes gradient dispersion, preserves stationary Killing energy to parts in $10^8$ and axial angular momentum to parts in $10^{13}$, lowers Kerr tidal-arc density error and azimuthal roughness below equal-runtime Monte Carlo ensembles, matches apparent horizon mass and constraint norms identically, and scales past two hundred thousand packets with nearly seventyfold lower compute cost per cycle than high-resolution continuum moments.

Three natural extensions remain for future work. Dynamic mid-simulation repartitioning between packet clouds and local continuum velocity bases would extend hybrid flexibility to flows that transition dynamically between free streaming and dense shock formation within a single evolution. Coupling fourth-order symplectic time integration to curved-metric packet transport could push stationary energy conservation toward machine precision over secular orbital timescales, while combining dynamical spacetime deposition with full collisional radiation-hydrodynamic feedback opens the door to self-consistent compact-binary merger and neutrino-transport simulations.
