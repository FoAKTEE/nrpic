\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Relativistic kinetic matter governs diverse compact-object environments, from dense stellar clusters and tidal debris around Kerr black holes to neutrino transport and marginally collisional plasmas in gravitational collapse \cite{1985ApJ...298...34S,Andreasson:2011ng,Acuna-Cardenas:2021nkj}. Across these systems the dimensionless Knudsen number varies by many orders of magnitude. Cold collapse and dilute galactic nuclei stream freely along timelike geodesics with vanishing two-body scattering, whereas dense relativistic outflows and shock layers relax rapidly toward local Maxwell-J{\"u}ttner equilibrium. Bridging both limits inside a dynamical three-plus-one spacetime requires accurate phase-space transport without excessive sampling noise, grid diffusion, or runaway computational cost.

Two numerical paradigms tackle kinetic transport in general relativity from opposite directions. Particle-in-cell (PIC) methods sample the six-dimensional phase space with finite-mass characteristics pushed along geodesics and deposited onto an adaptive spacetime mesh \cite{Wu:2026nrpic,Galishnikova:2025pfi,2026ApJS..283...27S}. Symplectic midpoint integration along individual characteristics preserves Killing invariants in stationary spacetimes and introduces zero momentum diffusion across neighbors. Finite Monte Carlo particle counts nonetheless seed Poisson sampling noise in smooth density profiles unless large ensembles are evolved. Conversely, meshless Lagrangian-basis moving-finite-volume (MFM) closures discretize local velocity space with realizable Gaussian or top-hat basis functions coupled by exact one-sided Riemann fluxes \cite{Hopkins:2026lbv,Hopkins:2015mfc}. Moving elements eliminate statistical sampling noise and capture continuum shocks seamlessly, yet warm shearing flows in curved geometry expose three liabilities: neighbor-cell gradient reconstruction disperses sharp cold streams across elements, basis covariances broaden under curved-metric tidal shear, and repeated nearest-neighbor searches dominate graphics-processor runtime at large element counts.

Here we construct a hybrid symplectic-cubature and asymptotic-preserving MFM solver for the general-relativistic Boltzmann-Vlasov system inside AthenaK \cite{2026ApJS..283...27S,Zhu:2024utz}. In the collisional and intermediate-Knudsen regime, Lagrangian velocity-basis moments advance on a moving partition coupled via safeguarded-Newton one-sided phase-space fluxes, a relativistic Synge-equation-of-state HLLC Riemann solver \cite{Mignone:2005ns,Synge:1957}, and energy-exact Maxwell-J{\"u}ttner Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) relaxation \cite{Anderson:1974,Israel:1963}. In the collisionless regime, each populated velocity basis expands once at initialization into fifth-degree Stroud cubature packets \cite{Stroud:1971} rotated by a low-discrepancy Halton orientation sequence \cite{Halton:1960}, then evolves along exact characteristic trajectories under the implicit symplectic midpoint pusher \cite{Wu:2026nrpic}. Three findings emerge from tests spanning special-relativistic shocks, Kerr tidal disruption, and full-relativity collapse:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Eliminating neighbor-grid collapse under adaptive smoothing-length ratios, packing Kokkos kernel functors below constant-memory launch thresholds, and replacing Brent iterations with analytical Newton steps cuts continuum GPU runtime sharply while preserving exact finite-volume parity.
\item Across four decades of collisionality in relativistic shock tubes, the asymptotic-preserving moment scheme recovers free-streaming collisionless dispersion at zero optical depth and matches the analytical Taub relativistic Rankine-Hugoniot jump in the hydrodynamic limit.
\item For collisionless curved-space flows, deterministic Stroud-cubature packets preserve Kerr Killing invariants cleanly, outperform equal-cost Monte Carlo PIC on tidal-arc density accuracy, recover the irreducible mass $M_{\rm AH}$ of the apparent horizon and the Hamiltonian constraint evolution in full three-plus-one Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse \cite{Oppenheimer:1939ue,Staley:2011ss}, and scale cleanly past one hundred thousand elements.
\end{enumerate}
