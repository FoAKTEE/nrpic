\title{High-order symplectic, metric-conservative, and asymptotic-preserving formulation of general-relativistic kinetic theory}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Gravitational collapse, relativistic star clusters, dark-matter spikes and photon rings around spinning black holes, binary mergers, and transitional neutrino or plasma flows all involve kinetic matter coupled to dynamically curved spacetime. Across these environments, the collisional mean free path spans many orders of magnitude: collisionless streams develop multi-valued phase-space caustics inaccessible to fluid closures, while frequent scattering drives dense shocks toward local thermodynamic equilibrium and stalls standard Monte Carlo particle schemes. Accurate nonlinear studies across both limits require numerical tools free of secular orbit dephasing, curved-metric energy leakage, and temporal coupling bias. We present a unified general-relativistic kinetic formulation built on the performance-portable AthenaK adaptive-mesh framework and coupled to three-dimensional Z4c spacetime evolution in moving-puncture gauge. The algorithm combines fourth-order symplectic tracking for massive particles and massless photons with smooth, memory-neutral high-order metric reconstruction, metric-conservative and positivity-preserving source filtering across refinement boundaries, causal stage-accurate spacetime coupling with multi-rate subcycling, symmetry-preserving deterministic sampling, and asymptotic-preserving collisional closures that bridge free streaming to optically thick shocks at constant cost per step. We validate the framework across four decades of optical depth, near-extremal spinning black hole zoom-whirl orbits and unstable light-ring separatrices, static-mesh relativistic clusters, spherical and prolate spindle collapse, and the equilibrium, migration, collapse, and dispersal sequence of relativistic star clusters. Highlighting its broad reach across strong-field nonlinear gravity, we track binary black hole formation inside a collisionless cluster and resolve self-consistent gravitational back-reaction, black hole recoil, apparent-horizon mass growth, spin-down, and quadrupolar gravitational radiation from massive particles plunging into spinning black holes. Compared with baseline second-order particle-in-cell methods, this unified framework cuts strong-field trajectory and conservation errors by orders of magnitude while accelerating three-dimensional dynamical evolutions. It provides an accurate, general-purpose computational foundation for relativistic kinetic astrophysics and numerical relativity.
\end{abstract}
