\begin{abstract}
Relativistic kinetic matter around black holes and inside gravitational collapse spans many orders of magnitude in collisional mean free path. Collisionless phase-space streaming forms fine orbital caustics that defeat fluid closures, while frequent binary scattering drives rapid relaxation toward local thermodynamic equilibrium and stalls standard Monte Carlo particle schemes. We formulate, analyze, and validate a unified general-relativistic kinetic algorithm inside AthenaK on adaptive three-plus-one Z4c spacetimes. For collisionless dynamics of both massive particles and null rays, a canonical two-stage fourth-order Gauss--Legendre symplectic pusher in Darboux phase-space coordinates pairs with three-level quadratic metric time interpolation and a zero-overhead twice-continuously-differentiable six-node quintic Hermite spatial metric reconstruction fitting within four ghost cells. For stress-energy coupling, a self-adjoint metric-weighted flux-conservative filter with harmonic face conductance preserves constant physical equilibria, proper-volume integrals across arbitrary adaptive refinement jumps, and unconditional positivity in both directional sweeps and an unsplit seven-point stencil with boundary-only reflux storage. Causal stage-accurate source reconstruction removes frozen-matter coupling bias, permitting three-stage strong-stability-preserving spacetime steps at high Courant numbers alongside multi-rate matter subcycling and a coupled puncture-split constraint solver. For collisional regimes, moving-finite-volume velocity bases with boosted one-sided Riemann integrals blend asymptotically into a Synge relativistic hydrodynamic limit, complemented in packet mode by a conservative Cell-BGK collision operator driven by a globally dominating Gamma-mixture Maxwell--J\"uttner sampler and exact convex four-momentum projection. Benchmarks spanning four decades of optical depth, near-extremal Kerr zoom-whirl and light-ring orbits, six-level static-mesh clusters, dynamical relativistic cluster and binary collapse, and massive-particle black hole back-reaction achieve order-of-magnitude gains in accuracy and single-accelerator throughput relative to baseline second-order methods.
\end{abstract}
