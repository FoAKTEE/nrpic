\begin{abstract}
Kinetic matter in strong gravitational fields spans two disparate regimes that challenge any single discretization: collisionless phase-space streaming with sharp orbital caustics and collisional relativistic plasma near local thermodynamic equilibrium. Meshless moving-finite-volume moment closures remove sampling noise across finite mean free paths, whereas symplectic particle integration preserves orbit invariants in collisionless spacetimes without neighbor diffusion. We combine both approaches in a unified GPU-accelerated general-relativistic solver built atop the AthenaK numerical-relativity framework. For collisional and transitional flows, an asymptotic-preserving finite-volume scheme evolves covariant velocity-space bases under exact one-sided Riemann integrals blended continuously into a Synge-equation-of-state relativistic HLLC fluid solver and energy-exact Maxwell-J{\"u}ttner relaxation. Eliminating neighbor-grid collapse under extreme shear, removing device-host launch synchronizations, and replacing bracketed root searches with analytical safeguarded Newton iterations accelerates this continuum solver substantially while matching analytical reference solutions to rounding precision. Whenever collisions vanish, warm velocity bases expand into quasi-random spatial orientations of fifth-degree Stroud cubature packets pushed by an implicit symplectic midpoint integrator, preserving orbital energy and angular momentum cleanly and beating ten-thousand-particle random sampling in tidal-stream fidelity at a fraction of the cost of a full continuum solve. Benchmarks spanning special-relativistic shock tubes across four decades of optical depth, a sheared cloud orbiting a rapidly spinning Kerr black hole, and full three-plus-one adaptive-mesh Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse establish smooth physical transitions, strong scaling beyond one hundred thousand phase-space elements, and high efficiency on a single graphics accelerator.
\end{abstract}
