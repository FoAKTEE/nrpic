NEW OPTIONS, <particles> block, Newtonian mode (pusher = newton)
1. Fourier Poisson solver on a periodic uniform mesh (Track B, Laplacian order):
     newton_poisson    = fft        multigrid (default) | fft
     newton_fft_symbol = spectral   spectral: Phi_k = -four_pi_G rho_k/k^2
                                    seven_point: the symbol of the seven-point Laplacian
                                    (the multigrid solution to rounding, no iteration)
   Needs: periodic in all directions, no refinement, one pack, cells per direction a
   product of 2, 3, 5, 7 (the directions may differ); MeshBlocks stay cubes with a
   power-of-two size (96^3 cells: MeshBlocks of 32^3, not 48^3); the <gravity> block
   stays in the input. Columns 48 and 50 of <basename>.newton.hst are zero.
   Example:
     python3 problems/newton/make_ic.py wave --out wave32.nrp --n 32
     athena_cpu -i problems/newton/periodic_fft.athinput particles/particle_file=wave32.nrp -d RUN
   Tested: T1 (seven_point equals the multigrid run to 2.2e-15 of the box at every
   snapshot through shell crossing; spectral equals the numpy scheme of the code to
   3.9e-16; a mesh of 48 x 80 x 112 cells to 1.8e-12 of the largest acceleration); T2,
   Track B's pancake against Zel'dovich (largest sheet error at a = 2: n = 16 3.28e-4,
   n = 32 9.55e-5, n = 64 4.58e-5, that is 17, 14.6 and 7.7 times below the multigrid
   runs of the track and within 13 and 6 per cent of cosmo/newton/pm.py at n = 32, 64;
   after the first crossings the gain is 1.2 to 5). Cost on a card with 2e6 particles:
   0.14 s per step at 256^3 (7.3 GiB), 0.44 s at 384^3 (22.6 GiB).
   Not tested: unequal cell sizes in the three directions with moving particles;
   meshes above 384^3; several packs (refused).

     newton_ngroup         = 1      number of groups (at most 8)

     newton_group1_tag_min = -1     first and last tag (position in the particle file;
     newton_group1_tag_max = -1     -1 or absent: no bound)
     newton_group1_x1 = 24.0  newton_group1_x2 = 24.0  newton_group1_x3 = 24.0
                                    reference point of the nearest image (default: the
                                    middle of the mesh)
   Output <basename>.newton.g<g>.hst, 62 columns: number, mass, centroid and its
   velocity; I_ij about the centroid in comoving coordinates with two derivatives in

ANSWERS WITHOUT A NEW OPTION
- Which energy (Track A): column 6 of <basename>.newton.hst is W gathered at the
  particles, column 7 the mesh sum. Neither is an invariant unless the deposit and the
  gather kernel are the same function. With the defaults (linear cloud, Catmull-Rom)
  the Layzer-Irvine constant drifts by 5e-3 of max a|W| at a shell crossing, the same
  at half the step (T7); with deposit_shape = pcs and interp = bspline it holds to
  3.7e-6 (embedded ellipsoid, T4d). For an energy budget use the second pair.
- Self-force (Track D): a single particle feels its own cloud: zero at a mesh vertex
  and at a cell centre, away from the vertex (slope 1.80 G m/dx^3, e-folding 0.75
  (dx^3/Gm)^1/2), towards the cell centre, up to 0.97 G m/dx^2; 5.9 times smaller
  with pcs and bspline (T8). newton_pm_mass (release core-v0.4) is not deposited and
  has none: in Track D's infall the turnaround mass follows the closed form within 0.7
  per cent at every snapshot for a seed at a vertex and at a cell centre alike (T5).
- Refined meshes (core's deferred item): on one static level the multigrid solver
  converges and gives the run of the uniform fine mesh where the matter is; a ball that
  crosses the level boundary keeps its momentum to 5e-5 M v and its energy to 4e-4,
  five times the uniform coarse mesh (T6c). Example:
     python3 problems/newton/make_ic.py ball --out ball.nrp --center 0.05 -0.05 0.03 \
        --radius 0.7 --mass 0.343 --spacing 0.03125 --ntracer 0 \
        --velocity -0.2 0.15 -0.1 --omega 0.2 -0.3 0.6
     athena_cpu -i problems/newton/isolated_smr.athinput particles/particle_file=ball.nrp particles/dt=0.002 -d RUN
  This example on processors equals the card run of T6c to 2.2e-13 over 20 steps (T12).
  Not tested: more than one level, periodic refined meshes, adaptive refinement, the
  Fourier solver there (refused).

PROPERTY OF THE SCHEME TO KNOW (newtonian_mode.md section 6 and 7.6)
A cold lattice with fewer than one particle per cell and direction, commensurate with
the mesh, is pushed as a whole by the self-forces of its particles: momentum change
2.1e-3 M v at one particle per eight cells, 1.15e-2 with a lattice of two cells, in the
first tenth of a free-fall time, against 1e-5 with one or more particles per cell and
direction (T6, T6b, T6c). It appears on uniform meshes too. Sample matter with at least
one particle per cell and direction of the finest level it occupies.

NOT IN THESE EXECUTABLES (REQUESTS_NEWTON2.md section 1): a fourth-order Laplacian on
isolated or refined meshes; the special-relativistic Newtonian push; snapshots at chosen
times; the Newtonian mode on more than one rank.
