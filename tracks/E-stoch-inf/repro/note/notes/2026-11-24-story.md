# 2026-11-24: the story of the note (skill Sec. 10.1, steps 1-3)

## Step 1. The story

Central question: can an independent numerical-relativity code, given only what two papers
print, reproduce their stochastic-inflation results, and what does the attempt teach about
the papers' statements?

Turning points:
1. The equations survive computer algebra: the sources are gauge independent and leave the
   constraints unsourced at first order. The misprints are many but none reaches the code.
2. Lattice paper: every observable that linear theory fixes (background, spectra, freezing,
   constraint orders, extrinsic curvature hierarchy, convergence) is reproduced. The one
   quantity that is not, the one-point bands, disagrees with the paper's own spectra by the
   same factor in both scenarios. Constraint levels follow from that factor and Z4c.
3. Lattice paper, backreaction: level 2 against level 0 is near one part in ten thousand
   as the paper's figure shows; its text's bound fails at the top shells in USR, as in the
   paper's own figure. Our level-1 curve measures a scheme difference (review on Fable).
4. One-patch paper: its figures are matched only by a background integrated with forward
   Euler at the step the paper names, and by the Ito reading of its printed equation, not by
   the Stratonovich reading it names. The Ito term carries alpha^5, and the USR noise
   amplitude of its appendix holds only if dR/dN is dropped.

Single picture to keep: what linear theory fixes is reproduced to the precision of the
lattice; what depends on unstated numerical choices (one-point normalisation, background
step, stochastic reading) is where the reproduction and the papers part.

## Step 2. 300-word version

Stochastic inflation adds the quantum noise of modes that cross a coarse-graining scale to
the classical evolution of long-wavelength fields. Launay, Rigopoulos and Shellard wrote
these sources for the full Einstein equations (Paper I) and evolved them in a BSSN code on a
lattice for a slow-roll and an ultra-slow-roll potential (Paper II). Paper I also integrates
a one-patch Langevin system for the lapse and the e-folds in the uniform-field gauge.

We reproduce both papers in their own set-up. The lattice part runs in AthenaK with the
Z4c system, an inflaton and the stochastic sources; the one-patch part with a reference
integrator of the Langevin pair. The authors' code and random numbers are not public, so the
comparison is statistical, against the linear (Mukhanov-Sasaki) solution, and against the
numbers the papers print.

On the lattice, the background, the spectra of the curvature perturbation and of the
extrinsic curvature, the freezing, the second order of the Hamiltonian constraint and its
first order under a flipped source, and the convergence with resolution all agree with
Paper II and with linear theory. Paper II's one-point bands are about three to four times
wider than its own spectra imply in both scenarios; ours agree with the spectra. Its stated
bound on the backreaction holds at large scales but not at the shortest scales of the
ultra-slow-roll run, as in its own figure. The momentum constraint is first order where the
shortest modes enter with four cells per wavelength.

For the one-patch system, Paper I's figures follow from a background integrated by forward
Euler at a step of a hundredth of an e-fold, which carries the field over a plateau where the
exact solution stops, and from the Ito reading of the printed equation. The Ito correction
carries the fifth power of the lapse, and the ultra-slow-roll noise amplitude is smaller than
printed by the square of the window parameter over three. Seventeen misprints are listed.

## Step 3. 1000-word version

**Introduction.** The statistics of rare, large curvature perturbations decide the abundance
of primordial black holes. Stochastic inflation captures the quantum diffusion that shapes
these tails: modes that cross a coarse-graining scale feed noise into the long-wavelength
dynamics. The usual form keeps only homogeneous patches. Launay, Rigopoulos and Shellard
derived stochastic sources for every Einstein equation in the 3+1 form, built only from the
comoving curvature perturbation and the time derivatives of the window (Paper I), and solved
them in numerical relativity on a lattice for a slow-roll (SR) and an ultra-slow-roll (USR)
scenario (Paper II). Paper I also integrates a one-patch Langevin pair for the lapse and the
e-folds. Neither code nor random numbers are public. A reproduction therefore tests what the
papers print: equations, set-up, figures and statements. We carried it out with AthenaK
(Z4c, an inflaton, the stochastic sources) and a reference integrator for the one-patch
system. Results: (i) the equations; (ii) the lattice observables fixed by linear theory;
(iii) the one-point bands and constraint levels; (iv) the backreaction; (v) the one-patch
figures and the numerical choices that produce them.

**Stochastic sources.** A field is split by a smooth window W_k at the scale k_sigma = sigma a H.
The coarse-grained curvature perturbation W R obeys the Mukhanov-Sasaki (MS) equation with a
source S_R built from W', W'' and R. The sources of the field momentum, of K and of the
trace-free extrinsic curvature are S_R times background factors. Computer algebra confirms
that these leave the linearised Hamiltonian and momentum constraints unsourced in five gauge
families with arbitrary gauge functions, and that flipping the sign of any one source breaks
them at first order. Seventeen misprints are found; none enters the code.

**Lattice set-up.** We use Paper II's potentials, parameters, box (one window wavelength),
cut-off (a quarter of the Nyquist number), window (sigma = 1, s = 5), geodesic slicing,
sixth-order stencils, fourth-order Runge-Kutta, no dissipation, a 256^3 lattice and the
smaller lattices of its appendix. Five differences remain: Z4c without damping instead of
BSSN; level 0 of the backreaction in the main runs (Paper II used level 2); the noise mode
equation per shell with the continuum wave number; a later Bunch-Davies start of the
highest SR shells; and the form of the level-0 background. Each acts at second order or at
the shortest scales.

**Lattice results.** The box means follow the Friedmann solution to rounding in SR and to
the integration error in USR, with the slow-roll parameters of Paper II's first figure. The
spectra of R^NR rise as modes enter the window and freeze at the MS spectrum; the shells
agree with the realisation's linear power to the stencil error at the top and to far less
at large scales. The K spectra freeze in SR and decay in USR; anisotropic and isotropic parts
are comparable during the crossings, and the anisotropic part decays afterwards in SR; in USR
the hierarchy is absent during the plateau and returns after it. The constraints converge;
the Hamiltonian constraint is second order in the noise, the momentum constraint first order
in the relative measure, and a flipped momentum source makes both first order.

**Where the lattice results part.** Paper II's one-point bands of phi, Pi, K and R are about
three to four times wider than ours in both scenarios, while its spectra agree with ours in
level. The linear normalisation fixes the one-point width from the spectrum, so the factor
lies in Paper II's real-space normalisation or in its plotting, not in the shared physics;
we cannot tell which. The same factor accounts for most of the gap in the constraint levels
(second order: its square). The momentum constraint has a first-order part where the
shortest modes enter with four cells per wavelength; with about thirteen cells it is gone.

**Backreaction.** Level 2 lets the coefficients of the R equation follow the local fields.
Against level 0 it changes the final power by about one part in ten thousand with no sign
(USR), rising toward the cut-off where the text's bound fails, as in Paper II's figure. SR
waits for a continued run. Our level-1 curve measures two integrations of one background,
not a backreaction; a review of the code on Fable showed this and found no defect.

**One-patch system.** The background crosses from a flat plateau to a slope. Integrated to
convergence it stops above the junction (the plateau stop); forward Euler at a step of a
hundredth of an e-fold carries it over at the e-fold Paper I quotes. The noise grows on the
plateau as e^{3N}, so without the crossing half the paths blow up. On the Euler background,
the Ito reading of the printed equation at the same step reproduces the widths, skewness,
kurtosis and tails of Paper I's four figures; the Stratonovich reading, which Paper I
names, is wider and more skewed. Ito's lemma gives alpha^5 in the drift, not alpha^3. The
USR noise amplitude of Paper I's appendix is smaller by sigma^2/3 when dR/dN is kept.

**Summary.** What linear theory fixes is reproduced; where the papers depend on choices
they do not state, the reproduction says which choice their figures require. Pending:
the 256^3 rungs of convergence, the SR level-2 curve, the SR constraint panels and the SR
sign flip, all waiting for continued runs; the fresh verifier's check of the one-patch
findings.

## Step 4. Reviewers

This session has no tool to start subagents (no Agent or Task tool is listed), so the
parallel reviewers of step 4 were not dispatched. In their place I read the 1000-word
version once as a first-year graduate student; spots where such a reader gets lost, and the
revision:
- "level 0, 1, 2" is used before it is defined: define the levels in Sec. II in one sentence
  each.
- "one-point bands" is jargon: say "the spread of the field values over the lattice cells".
- "Ito reading" vs "Stratonovich reading": one sentence on what each means for an equation
  with multiplicative noise, before the result.
- The plateau stop needs one sentence of physics: on a flat plateau the velocity decays as
  e^{-3N}, so the field travels a finite distance.
- Too many findings for one Introduction list: group them as (i)-(v) as above.
