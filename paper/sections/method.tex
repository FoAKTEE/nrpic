\section{The particle-mesh method}\label{sec:method}

Coupled Einstein-Vlasov evolution advances smooth curvature fields on an Eulerian mesh alongside discrete trajectories over a shared global time step $\Delta t$, set by the Courant limit of the finest cell spacing $\Delta x$. The numerical-relativity particle-in-cell (NRPIC) method \cite{Wu:2026nrpic} links both representations in a staggered cycle, analogous to electromagnetic particle-in-cell codes where Maxwell's equations take the place of the spacetime solver \cite{1988csup.book.....H,1991ppcs.book.....B}. Finite sampling with a mean count $\Nppc$ of macroparticles per cell introduces sampling noise, discrete fluctuations of relative amplitude $\Nppc^{-1/2}$. Each stage of the cycle preserves orbital time-reversal symmetry, conserves deposited weight across refinement boundaries, and keeps grid sources smooth at modest $\Nppc$.

One cycle from $t^n$ to $t^{n+1} = t^n + \Delta t$ runs through six stages in sequence (Fig.~\ref{fig:cycle}). First, $\alpha$, $\beta^i$, and $\gamma_{ij}$ at $t^n$ are saved across all active and ghost cells. Second, four fourth-order Runge--Kutta stages advance the spacetime variables to $t^{n+1}$ while holding the sources $(E, S_i, S_{ij})$ from $t^n$ fixed. Third, a geodesic pusher---an update advancing each macroparticle's position and momentum across $\Delta t$---pushes every particle from $t^n$ to $t^{n+1}$ in the metric averaged between both endpoints. Fourth, lapse excision removes particles deep inside black holes where $\alpha(t^n, \bm{x}_p^n) < \aexc$ for a threshold $\aexc$. Out-of-domain or nonfinite states are discarded, and surviving particles are reassigned to their enclosing blocks. Fifth, stress-energy deposition, the assignment of each macroparticle's mass-energy, momentum, and stress to nearby cells, deposits $(E, S_i, S_{ij})$ at $t^{n+1}$, filters the ten arrays, and evaluates the constraints. Sixth, diagnostics run and the adaptive mesh refines or coarsens where curvature gradients cross thresholds. After any regrid, metric arrays, block ownership, and sources are rebuilt on the new leaf cells while $(m_p, x_p^i, u_{i,p})$ stay unchanged.

\subsection{Geodesic pushers}\label{sec:pushers}

Collisionless equilibria and close black hole passages require trajectory updates free of secular drift in orbital invariants. The implicit midpoint pusher and the spacetime Boris pusher \cite{Wu:2026nrpic} store $x_p^i$ and $u_{i,p}$ at integer time levels, reach second-order accuracy in $\Delta t$, and preserve time-reversal symmetry through midpoint evaluation. Each queries the geometry solely through a point-evaluation function returning $\alpha$, $\beta^i$, $\gamma_{ij}$, and their first spatial derivatives, so identical code runs on analytic metrics, uniform lattices, and adaptive meshes. Fixed-point iteration updates the six endpoint variables $(x_p^{i,n+1}, u_{i,p}^{n+1})$ until the largest component change falls below a tolerance.

The implicit midpoint pusher evaluates the characteristic equations at the phase-space midpoint (with the particle index $p$ suppressed),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:midpoint_state}
\bar{x}^i = \frac{x^{i,n} + x^{i,n+1}}{2}, \qquad
\bar{u}_i = \frac{u_i^n + u_i^{n+1}}{2}.
\end{equation}
Spatial reconstructions at $t^n$ and $t^{n+1}$ are evaluated at $\bar{\bm{x}}$ and averaged to obtain $\bar{\alpha}$, $\bar{\beta}^i$, $\bar{\gamma}_{ij}$, and their spatial derivatives. Matrix inversion of $\bar{\gamma}_{ij}$ yields $\bar{\gamma}^{ij}$, $\bar{u}^i = \bar{\gamma}^{ij}\bar{u}_j$, and $\bar{W} = \sqrt{1 + \bar{\gamma}^{ij}\bar{u}_i\bar{u}_j}$, advancing the trajectory by the midpoint update,
\begin{align}\label{eq:midpoint_update}
\frac{x^{i,n+1} - x^{i,n}}{\Delta t} &= \frac{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\gamma}^{ij}\bar{u}_j}{\bar{W}} - \bar{\beta}^i, \nonumber\\
\frac{u_i^{n+1} - u_i^n}{\Delta t} &= -\bar{W}\partial_i\bar{\alpha} + \bar{u}_j\partial_i\bar{\beta}^j + \frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2\bar{W}}\bar{u}^j\bar{u}^k\partial_i\bar{\gamma}_{jk},
\end{align}
where $\bar{u}^j\bar{u}^k\partial_i\bar{\gamma}_{jk} = -\bar{u}_j\bar{u}_k\partial_i\bar{\gamma}^{jk}$. Fixed by the coordinate metric alone, the midpoint update conserves any momentum $u_i$ conjugate to a cyclic coordinate ($\partial_i = 0$) to rounding error \cite{Bacchini:2020cze}.

The spacetime Boris pusher projects momentum onto the normal-observer tetrad, pairing $e_{(0)}^\mu = n^\mu$ with a spatial triad $e^{(m)}_i$ of three mutually perpendicular unit covectors on the slice \cite{Olivares:2021cfa,Boyeneni:2025tsx,Wu:2026nrpic}. The triad and its inverse $e^i_{(m)}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triad_def}
\gamma_{ij} = \delta_{mn} e^{(m)}_i e^{(n)}_j, \qquad
e^i_{(m)} e^{(n)}_i = \delta^n_m,
\end{equation}
with Kronecker deltas $\delta_{mn}$ and $\delta^n_m$. Projection defines the tetrad momentum $u_{(m)} = e^i_{(m)} u_i$ (so $u_i = e^{(n)}_i u_{(n)}$) and $W = \sqrt{1 + \delta^{mn} u_{(m)} u_{(n)}}$. Along a trajectory with $u^j = \gamma^{jk} u_k$, coordinate velocity $v^j$, and convective derivative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coord_vel}
\frac{\dd}{\dd t} = \partial_t + v^j \partial_j, \qquad
v^j = \frac{\alpha u^j}{W} - \beta^j,
\end{equation}
differentiating $u_i = e^{(n)}_i u_{(n)}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triad_chain}
e^{(n)}_i \frac{\dd u_{(n)}}{\dd t} = \frac{\dd u_i}{\dd t} - u_{(n)}\bigl(\partial_t e^{(n)}_i + v^j \partial_j e^{(n)}_i\bigr).
\end{equation}
Spatial differentiation of $\gamma_{jk} = \delta_{mn} e^{(m)}_j e^{(n)}_k$ rewrites the metric-gradient term in the characteristic equations as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric_triad_id}
\frac{1}{2} u^j u^k \partial_i \gamma_{jk} = u^j u_{(n)} \partial_i e^{(n)}_j.
\end{equation}
With the momentum-contracted triad gradients
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triad_gradients}
c_{jk} = u_{(n)} \partial_j e^{(n)}_k, \qquad
c^t_k = u_{(n)} \partial_t e^{(n)}_k,
\end{equation}
inserting the characteristic equations, contracting with $e^i_{(m)}$, and passing to normal-observer proper time $\dd\hat{t} = \alpha\,\dd t$ yield the tetrad equation of motion,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tetrad_eom}
\frac{\dd u_{(m)}}{\dd\hat{t}} = \Et_{(m)} + \frac{1}{W}\epsilon_{(m)(n)(k)} u_{(n)} \Bt_{(k)},
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{(m)(n)(k)}$ is the Levi-Civita symbol. Here the momentum-contracted gravito-electric and gravito-magnetic fields $\Et_{(m)}$ and $\Bt_{(k)}$ read
\begin{align}\label{eq:gravito_em}
\alpha \Et_{(m)} &= e^i_{(m)}\bigl(-W\partial_i\alpha + u_j\partial_i\beta^j + \beta^j c_{ji} - c^t_i\bigr), \nonumber\\
\Bt_{(k)} &= \epsilon_{(k)(m)(n)} e^i_{(m)} e^j_{(n)} c_{ij},
\end{align}
representing the tetrad field strengths $\partial_\mu e^{(m)}_\nu - \partial_\nu e^{(m)}_\mu$ contracted with momentum \cite{Olivares:2021cfa,Boyeneni:2025tsx}.

Plasma particle-in-cell codes integrate the Lorentz force with the Boris step \cite{boris1970relativistic,1991ppcs.book.....B}, a velocity-space counterpart of kick-drift-kick leapfrog that brackets a magnetic rotation at constant $W$ between two half-step electric kicks. In the tetrad equation of motion, $\Et_{(m)}$ carries the lapse, shift, and temporal triad gradients that change $W$, whereas $\Bt_{(k)}$ carries the spatial triad curl and rotates $u_{(m)}$ at fixed $W$. Both fields depend on momentum, making the update implicit. Each fixed-point iteration forms $u_{(m)}^n = e^i_{(m)}(t^n, \bm{x}^n) u_i^n$ and $u_{(m)}^{n+1} = e^i_{(m)}(t^{n+1}, \bm{x}^{n+1}) u_i^{n+1}$ and evaluates $\bar{\Et}_{(m)}$, $\bar{\Bt}_{(k)}$, and $\bar{W}$ at $\bar{\bm{x}}$ from their mean $\bar{u}_{(m)}$. With $\Delta\hat{t} = \bar{\alpha}\Delta t$ and $\bm{u}_{\rm tet} = (u_{(1)}, u_{(2)}, u_{(3)})$, momentum and position advance through the Boris rotation,
\begin{align}\label{eq:boris_rotation}
\bm{u}^{-} &= \bm{u}_{\rm tet}^n + \frac{\Delta\hat{t}}{2}\bar{\bm{\Et}}, \nonumber\\
\bm{\vartheta} &= \frac{\Delta\hat{t}}{2\bar{W}}\bar{\bm{\Bt}}, \nonumber\\
\bm{u}' &= \bm{u}^{-} + \bm{u}^{-} \times \bm{\vartheta}, \nonumber\\
\bm{u}^{+} &= \bm{u}^{-} + \frac{2(\bm{u}' \times \bm{\vartheta})}{1 + |\bm{\vartheta}|^2}, \nonumber\\
\bm{u}_{\rm tet}^{n+1} &= \bm{u}^{+} + \frac{\Delta\hat{t}}{2}\bar{\bm{\Et}}, \nonumber\\
\frac{x^{i,n+1} - x^{i,n}}{\Delta t} &= \frac{\bar{\alpha}\bar{e}^i_{(m)}\bar{u}_{(m)}}{\bar{W}} - \bar{\beta}^i.
\end{align}
After convergence, $u_{(m)}^{n+1}$ converts back to $u_i^{n+1} = e^{(m)}_i(t^{n+1}, \bm{x}^{n+1}) u_{(m)}^{n+1}$.

Free motion in polar coordinates has straight-line solutions, yet a finite step that evaluates unit-vector rotation at the midpoint and projects at the endpoints makes an error that Cartesian axes avoid. The spatial triad is such a set of unit vectors, fixed by $\gamma_{ij}$ only up to a local rotation. Along a continuous geodesic, frame-rotation terms in $c_{jk}$ and $c^t_k$ cancel endpoint projections identically. Over a finite step $\Delta t$, midpoint evaluation leaves a second-order truncation error set by this local frame choice, the tetrad gauge. Two gauges are implemented here. The symmetric gauge uses the symmetric positive-definite square root of $\gamma_{ij}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric_gauge}
e^{(m)}_j = \bigl(\gamma^{1/2}\bigr)_{mj},
\end{equation}
from eigendecomposition, with spatial derivatives solved from the Sylvester equation for $\partial(\gamma^{1/2})$ in the eigenbasis. The Cholesky gauge uses the lower-triangular factor $L_{jm}$ satisfying $\gamma_{ij} = \delta^{mn} L_{im} L_{jn}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cholesky_gauge}
e^{(m)}_j = L_{jm}, \qquad L_{jm} = 0 \text{ for } j < m,
\end{equation}
equivalent to Gram--Schmidt orthonormalization of the coordinate basis in index order in closed form at lower cost. Reference~\cite{Wu:2026nrpic} leaves its tetrad gauge unstated. On its geodesic benchmarks (Sec.~\ref{sec:testparticles}), the symmetric gauge reproduces all published digits of the invariant errors for analytic spherical Kerr--Schild input. On the reconstructed Cartesian mesh, the Cholesky gauge in $(x,y,z)$ order reproduces all published digits. Permuted coordinate orderings and cross-paired gauges give distinct errors, while any triad construction that coincides with these two frames along the tested orbits remains consistent with the published digits.

\subsection{Metric reconstruction}\label{sec:reconstruction}

Geodesic integration on a mesh requires metric components and first spatial derivatives between cell centers with continuous values and gradients across cell faces. The mesh stores $\alpha$, $\beta^i$, and the six covariant components $\gamma_{ij}$ at cell centers, and the interpolated $3 \times 3$ spatial metric is inverted afterward to preserve symmetry.

Along each axis, a four-point cubic Hermite (Catmull--Rom) stencil spans two cell centers on each side of the bracketing interval at fractional offset $s \in [0,1)$ in units of the cell spacing $h$. Interpolation and differentiation evaluate the Hermite weights,
\begin{align}\label{eq:hermite_weights}
w_{-1}(s) &= \frac{-s + 2s^2 - s^3}{2}, \nonumber\\
w_{0}(s) &= \frac{2 - 5s^2 + 3s^3}{2}, \nonumber\\
w_{1}(s) &= \frac{s + 4s^2 - 3s^3}{2}, \nonumber\\
w_{2}(s) &= \frac{-s^2 + s^3}{2}, \nonumber\\
w'_a(s) &= \frac{1}{h}\frac{\dd w_a}{\dd s}.
\end{align}
Tensor products $w_a(s_1) w_b(s_2) w_c(s_3)$ over the $4^3$ surrounding cells reconstruct each field, while substituting $w'_a(s_i)$ along axis $i$ gives the exact polynomial derivative $\partial_i$. The stencil needs at least two ghost cells around every mesh block, and the runs here use more. Each macroparticle interpolates within its owning block, shifting the stencil inward by one cell to extrapolate the same cubic when an iterate nears the outer edge of the allocated cells. For standalone grid tests, the same routine samples an analytic metric on a virtual uniform lattice of cell centers of spacing $h$.

\subsection{Stress-energy deposition}\label{sec:deposition}

Einstein's equations require smooth grid stress-energy components whose proper-volume integrals equal the discrete particle sums. Each macroparticle carries a shape function, a localized profile of unit integral that sets its cloud width on the grid \cite{1988csup.book.....H,1991ppcs.book.....B}. On a uniform lattice of spacing $\Delta x$ and cell centers $\bm{x}_q$, tensor products of the first-order (cloud-in-cell) shape function $B_1$ define eight nonvanishing lattice weights $\tilde{\omega}_{qp}$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:cic_shape}
B_1(\xi) &= \max\bigl(0,\, 1 - |\xi|\bigr), \nonumber\\
\tilde{\omega}_{qp} &= \prod_{i=1}^{3} B_1\!\left(\frac{x_q^i - x_p^i}{\Delta x}\right),
\end{align}
which are nonnegative and sum to unity. Dividing the accumulated moments in leaf cell $C$ by its proper spatial volume $\sqrt{\gamma_C}\,\Delta V_C$, with cell-centered determinant $\gamma_C$ and coordinate volume $\Delta V_C$, yields the ten deposited sources,
\begin{align}\label{eq:deposited_sources}
\bigl(E,\, S_i,\, S_{ij}\bigr)_C &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma_C}\,\Delta V_C} \nonumber\\
&\quad \times \sum_{p=1}^{N} \omega_{Cp}\, m_p \left(W_p,\, u_{i,p},\, \frac{u_{i,p} u_{j,p}}{W_p}\right),
\end{align}
with dimensionless leaf-cell weight $\omega_{Cp}$.

At a coarse-fine mesh boundary, evaluating $B_1$ separately on each cell with its own width $\Delta x_C$ pairs unequal cloud widths on the two sides. For a straddling particle, those weights fail to sum to unity, so mesh integrals differ from the particle sums. A single representation level $\ell_p$ for each macroparticle $p$ restores unit total weight \cite{Myers:2021gfn,Wu:2026nrpic}. Its cloud lives on a virtual uniform lattice of spacing $\Delta x(\ell_p)$, producing eight unit-sum weights $\tilde{\omega}_{qp}$ on virtual cells $q$ of volume $\Delta V_q$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:deposition}). Those virtual weights map onto leaf cells $C$ of level $\ell_C$ and volume $\Delta V_C$ through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaf_weights}
\omega_{Cp} =
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \sum_{q \subset C} \tilde{\omega}_{qp}, & \ell_C \le \ell_p, \\[10pt]
\displaystyle \tilde{\omega}_{qp}\,\frac{\Delta V_C}{\Delta V_q}, & C \subset q,\ \ell_C > \ell_p.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Coarser leaf cells ($\ell_C \le \ell_p$) sum enclosed virtual weights, while finer leaf cells ($\ell_C > \ell_p$) share the virtual cell's coordinate density in proportion to volume. With $\ell_{\rm loc}(p)$ the finest level among leaf blocks owning the eight cell centers nearest $\bm{x}_p$, setting $\ell_p = \ell_{\rm loc}(p)$ places every cloud at the finest local level, where only the $\ell_C \le \ell_p$ branch occurs. This published rule \cite{Myers:2021gfn,Wu:2026nrpic} is used for every result in the paper. Because $\sum_C \omega_{Cp} = 1$ for every particle, summing proper-volume-weighted moments over all leaf cells yields the conservation identity,
\begin{align}\label{eq:conservation_identity}
\sum_{C} \bigl(E,\, S_i,\, S_{ij}\bigr)_C \sqrt{\gamma_C}\,\Delta V_C
&= \sum_{p=1}^{N} m_p \left(W_p,\, u_{i,p},\, \frac{u_{i,p} u_{j,p}}{W_p}\right),
\end{align}
to rounding error across arbitrary refinement boundaries. Enclosing blocks are found by geometric search, and cell updates accumulate via concurrent atomic additions.

Each refinement level halves $\Delta x$ and divides $\Nppc$ by eight, leaving fine cells under diffuse matter with far less than one particle on average. An optional level-capped deposition bounds $\ell_p$ through the level cap,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level_cap}
\ell_p = \min\bigl(\ell_{\rm loc}(p),\, \lcap\bigr),
\end{equation}
using either a fixed or density-adaptive ceiling $\lcap$, and recovers the published rule as $\lcap \to \infty$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:deposition}). Both branches of the leaf-weight mapping preserve $\sum_C \omega_{Cp} = 1$, so the conservation identity still holds to rounding error. Coarser clouds raise constraint residuals near forming black holes and shift horizon masses by a few tenths of a percent, more on lattices, yet bound refinement on randomly sampled matter (Sec.~\ref{sec:dust}). Every collapse result in Sec.~\ref{sec:selfgravity} uses the published rule.

Short-wavelength sampling fluctuations in $(E, S_i, S_{ij})$ are smoothed after deposition by $n_{\rm f}$ passes of a separable binomial filter with one-dimensional weights $(1/4, 1/2, 1/4)$ \cite{1991ppcs.book.....B,Wu:2026nrpic}. Ghost cells are filled before each pass by copying at equal level and by restriction and prolongation across refinement levels. Along each axis at wavenumber $k$, $n_{\rm f}$ binomial passes multiply Fourier modes by $\cos^{2n_{\rm f}}(k\Delta x/2)$ and add $n_{\rm f}\Delta x^2/2$ to the second spatial moment of every cloud. They widen every cloud, which acts as a gravitational softening, a short-range smoothing of the gravitational pull over the filter scale \cite{2008gady.book.....B}. For a self-gravitating equilibrium, this widening makes the deposited source less concentrated than the matter and lowers the proper-volume integral of $E$ slightly across nonuniform $\sqrt{\gamma}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:selfgravity}).

Plasma particle-in-cell codes cancel this second-moment widening by following the $n_{\rm f}$ binomial passes with one separable compensation pass \cite{1991ppcs.book.....B} of one-dimensional weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp_weights}
\bigl(-c,\, 1 + 2c,\, -c\bigr), \qquad c = \frac{n_{\rm f}}{4}.
\end{equation}
With $s = \sin^2(k\Delta x/2)$, the combined one-dimensional wavenumber response is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp_response}
F(k) = (1 - s)^{n_{\rm f}}(1 + n_{\rm f} s) = 1 - \mathcal{O}\bigl((k\Delta x)^4\bigr).
\end{equation}
This response equals unity to fourth order in $k\Delta x$, falls monotonically to zero at the Nyquist wavenumber $k = \pi/\Delta x$, cancels the second moment, and carries small negative sidelobes. Cluster evolutions and the randomly sampled prolate collapse in Sec.~\ref{sec:selfgravity} apply four binomial passes plus this compensation pass. Spherical dust collapse on a lattice retains one plain binomial pass \cite{Wu:2026nrpic}. With uniform $\sqrt{\gamma}$, both stencils conserve total weight on a single level, whereas mesh-level filtering across coarse-fine ghost zones is nonconservative once particles cross refinement boundaries (Sec.~\ref{sec:testparticles}).

\subsection{Spacetime evolution}\label{sec:spacetime}

Gravitational collapse and black hole mergers require a hyperbolic spacetime formulation that propagates curvature waves and damps constraint violations. We adopt AthenaK's solver \cite{2026ApJS..283...27S,Zhu:2024utz} for the Z4c formulation \cite{Bona:2003fj,Gundlach:2005eh,Hilditch:2012fp} in moving-puncture gauge, the standard coordinate choice for evolving black holes on a fixed Cartesian mesh without excising grid cells \cite{Campanelli:2005dd,Baker:2005vv,Alcubierre:2002kk,vanMeter:2006vi}. With $K = \gamma^{ij}K_{ij}$, Z4c evolves the conformal variables
\begin{align}\label{eq:z4c_vars}
\chi &= \gamma^{-1/3}, &
\tilde{\gamma}_{ij} &= \chi\gamma_{ij}, \nonumber\\
\tilde{A}_{ij} &= \chi\!\left(K_{ij} - \frac{1}{3}\gamma_{ij}K\right), &
\hat{K} &= K - 2\Theta,
\end{align}
alongside the contracted conformal connection $\tilde{\Gamma}^i$ and constraint-propagation scalar $\Theta$, damping the Hamiltonian and momentum constraint residuals $\Ham$ and $\Mom_i$ through $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$. Moving-puncture gauge combines $1+\log$ lapse slicing \cite{Bona:1994dr,Alcubierre:2002kk} with the integrated Gamma-driver shift condition \cite{Alcubierre:2002kk,Campanelli:2005dd,Baker:2005vv} damped at rate $\eta$ with shift speed parameter $\mu_S$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:puncture_gauge}
\partial_t \alpha &= \beta^i \partial_i \alpha - 2\alpha \hat{K}, \nonumber\\
\partial_t \beta^i &= \mu_S \tilde{\Gamma}^i - \eta \beta^i + \beta^j \partial_j \beta^i.
\end{align}
Cell-centered finite differences---sixth order on refined meshes and fourth order on uniform meshes---advance the state with Kreiss--Oliger dissipation, fourth-order Runge--Kutta integration, and Sommerfeld radiative outer boundaries \cite{Zhu:2024utz}.

AthenaK's Z4c module, previously coupled solely to magnetohydrodynamics \cite{Zhu:2024utz}, is extended here to receive the particle-deposited sources $(E, S_i, S_{ij})$ in the evolution equations and constraint residuals $\Ham$ and $\Mom_i$. An optional parabolic relaxation of the Hamiltonian constraint \cite{Duez:2002bn,Etienne:2024ncu,Wu:2026nrpic} adds diffusive damping to the conformal factor evolution,
\begin{align}\label{eq:parabolic_relaxation}
\partial_t \chi &= (\partial_t \chi)_{\rm Z4c} + \frac{c_H}{2}\chi^{-1/4}\Ham, \nonumber\\
\Ham &= {}^{(3)}R + \frac{2}{3}K^2 - \tilde{A}_{ij}\tilde{A}^{ij} - 16\pi E,
\end{align}
where $(\partial_t \chi)_{\rm Z4c}$ is the standard Z4c evolution term and ${}^{(3)}R$ is the Ricci scalar of $\gamma_{ij}$. Here the Hamiltonian-constraint relaxation length $c_H$ sums a constant and a term proportional to $\Delta x$ for stability across refinement levels.

\subsection{Adaptive mesh}\label{sec:amr}

Gravitational collapse and black hole mergers concentrate steep curvature gradients inside volumes far smaller than the ambient cluster or wave zone. AthenaK uses block-structured octree refinement, splitting cubic mesh blocks recursively into eight half-sized child blocks where finer resolution is required \cite{2020ApJS..249....4S,2026ApJS..283...27S}. Uniform and statically refined meshes enter as the limits without dynamic block splitting.

Dynamic refinement tracks the scalar monitor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refinement_monitor}
q = \alpha \chi^{-7/4},
\end{equation}
which rises sharply in strong-field regions as $\chi^{-7/4}$ grows near black holes \cite{Wu:2026nrpic}. With $\bm{\iota}_a$ the unit cell shift along axis $a \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $\Delta_a q_i = q_{i+\bm{\iota}_a} - q_{i-\bm{\iota}_a}$, each cell $i$ evaluates a L\"ohner-type second-difference ratio \cite{1987CMAME..61..323L,Wu:2026nrpic}, the refinement indicator,
\begin{align}\label{eq:refinement_indicator}
\varepsilon_i^2 &= \frac{\sum_{a,b=1}^{3} \bigl(\Delta_a q_{i+\bm{\iota}_b} - \Delta_a q_{i-\bm{\iota}_b}\bigr)^2}{\sum_{a,b=1}^{3} \bigl(|\Delta_a q_{i+\bm{\iota}_b}| + |\Delta_a q_{i-\bm{\iota}_b}| + \epsilon_0 q_{ab,i} + \delta_0\bigr)^2}, \nonumber\\
q_{ab,i} &= \sum_{\sigma,\tau=\pm 1} \bigl|q_{i+\sigma\bm{\iota}_a+\tau\bm{\iota}_b}\bigr|.
\end{align}
Here $\epsilon_0$ is a dimensionless filter weight and $\delta_0$ is a positive floor. Blocks refine when $\max_i \varepsilon_i$ exceeds an upper threshold and derefine below a lower threshold, while prescribed inner radii enforce a minimum level resolving diffuse matter at the initial particle-lattice scale. Through every regrid, macroparticles keep $(m_p, x_p^i, u_{i,p})$ unchanged without splitting or merging, and metric arrays, block ownership, and deposited sources are rebuilt on the new leaf cells.

\subsection{Numerical methods}\label{sec:numerical_methods}

End-to-end evolutions from initial equilibria through black hole formation rely on interior particle excision, horizon and mass diagnostics, constraint-satisfying initial data, and portable parallel execution.

Inside a black hole, $1+\log$ slicing drives $\alpha$ toward zero while signals remain causally trapped behind the horizon. Lapse excision \cite{Rosswog:2023nnl,Wu:2026nrpic} removes particles with $\alpha(t^n, \bm{x}_p^n) < \aexc$, clearing black hole interiors through this lapse criterion alone. On meshes with few cells per horizon radius, the lapse at cell centers next to the puncture stays at or above $\aexc$, so lapse excision alone leaves matter inside the black hole. A second rule, used only for black holes of a tenth of the total mass $M$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:selfgravity}), removes particles inside 0.8 of a found apparent horizon's smallest coordinate radius, measured from its center. This rule acts only after a successful search of the current run and ignores surfaces several times larger than the starting radius of the search. Black hole boundaries and irreducible masses are measured on apparent horizons, outermost closed two-surfaces on a slice whose outgoing orthogonal null geodesics have zero expansion. Apparent horizons are located with AthenaK's pseudospectral flow finder \cite{Gundlach:1997us,Zhu:2024utz}, searched every few cycles and stopped once the horizon-mass change between iterations falls below a tolerance. As distributed, the finder computes finite-difference metric derivatives on the active cells of each mesh block and interpolates that derivative array to the trial surface without filling its ghost zones across block faces. Here the metric derivatives on the trial surface are evaluated directly by differentiating the local Lagrange interpolant of the metric, whose ghost cells are filled. Its effect on a black hole of known mass is shown in Sec.~\ref{sec:selfgravity}. The finder's fixed flow-step constant, sized for a unit-mass black hole in code units, makes the first step for mass $m \ll M$ exceed the starting radius and end the search without a horizon. This constant is therefore reduced in proportion (by a factor of twenty for $m \simeq 0.1\,M$), after which the finder and the Misner--Sharp mass $M_{\rm MS}$ below agree to one percent.

An independent check is provided by the Misner--Sharp mass, the gravitational mass inside a coordinate sphere computed from the slice geometry alone,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:misner_sharp}
M_{\rm MS} = \frac{R}{2}\bigl(1 - \gamma^{ij}\partial_i R\,\partial_j R + R^2 K_T^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here $R$ is the areal radius of a coordinate sphere of proper area $4\pi R^2$ and $K_T$ is the tangential component of $K_{ij}$ on the sphere. In spherical symmetry, $2M_{\rm MS}/R = 1$ locates the apparent horizon and $M_{\rm MS}$ equals the black hole mass in vacuum. Values of $M_{\rm MS}$ are evaluated on saved slices independently of the finder. For origin-centered runs, central lapse $\alpha_c(t)$ and central energy density $E_c(t)$ average the eight central leaf cells, defining the central normal-observer proper time,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central_proper_time}
\tau_c(t) = \int_0^t \alpha_c(t')\,\dd t'.
\end{equation}
Coordinate-independent curvature is tracked via the Kretschmann scalar $I = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ of the Riemann tensor $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, decomposed through the Gauss, Codazzi, and Ricci equations with Einstein's equations for the two-normal projection \cite{Wu:2026nrpic},
\begin{align}\label{eq:kretschmann}
I &= G_{ijkl}G^{ijkl} - 4 C_{ijk}C^{ijk} + 4 Z_{ij}Z^{ij}, \nonumber\\
G_{ijkl} &= {}^{(3)}R_{ijkl} + K_{ik}K_{jl} - K_{il}K_{jk}, \nonumber\\
C_{ijk} &= D_i K_{jk} - D_j K_{ik}, \nonumber\\
Z_{ij} &= {}^{(3)}R_{ij} + K K_{ij} - K_{ik}K^k{}_j \nonumber\\
       &\quad - 8\pi\left[S_{ij} - \frac{1}{2}\gamma_{ij}(S - E)\right].
\end{align}
Here ${}^{(3)}R_{ijkl}$ and ${}^{(3)}R_{ij}$ are the Riemann and Ricci tensors of $\gamma_{ij}$, $S = \gamma^{ij}S_{ij}$, and indices are raised with $\gamma^{ij}$. This expression is checked against the analytic Schwarzschild profile.

Self-gravitating runs start on conformally flat, time-symmetric slices---surfaces of instantaneous rest with $K_{ij} = 0$, $\beta^i = 0$, and $\gamma_{ij} = \psi^4 \delta_{ij}$ for conformal factor $\psi$---where $\Mom_i = 0$ identically. Linear superposition of flat-space Poisson potentials $u_c$ for conformal density sources $\tilde{\rho}_c(\bm{x})$ solves $\Ham = 0$ via
\begin{align}\label{eq:initial_superposition}
\psi(\bm{x}) &= 1 + \sum_{c} u_c(\bm{x}), \nonumber\\
\nabla_{\rm flat}^2 u_c &= -2\pi \tilde{\rho}_c, \nonumber\\
E(\bm{x}) &= \psi(\bm{x})^{-5} \sum_{c} \tilde{\rho}_c(\bm{x}).
\end{align}
Components combine tabulated spherical equilibria, Gaussians, closed-form homogeneous prolate spheroids, and vacuum black hole punctures
\begin{equation}\label{eq:puncture_potential}
u_c(\bm{x}) = \frac{m_c}{2\rbar_c}
\end{equation}
of bare mass $m_c$ at isotropic distance $\rbar_c$. With $\sqrt{\gamma} = \psi^6$, equal-mass ensembles of rest-mass density $\rho_0$ have coordinate number density proportional to $\rho_0 \psi^6$. Cold spherical collapse places macroparticles on a mesh-aligned cubic lattice of spacing $h$, where the published rule \cite{Wu:2026nrpic} assigns rest mass $m_p = \rho_0(\bm{x}_p)\psi(\bm{x}_p)^6 h^3$ inside the surface. The number of lattice sites inside a sphere differs from its volume in cells by a surface fraction inversely proportional to the cell count across, giving a rest-mass offset at low $N$. Here a surface correction rescales $m_p$ in the outermost layer of width $h$ by a common factor so $\sum_p m_p$ equals the continuum rest mass $M_0$. Interior masses stay unchanged, and omitting this rescaling recovers the published rule (Sec.~\ref{sec:selfgravity}). Spherical clusters draw particles by randomized stratified sampling in radius and in both position and velocity angles, or by independent Monte Carlo draws in sampling comparisons. The mass-weighted mean of $u_{i,p}$ is subtracted so net cluster momentum vanishes, while prolate spheroids use equal-mass Monte Carlo sampling with reflection and half-turn symmetries.

All mesh and particle kernels run through Kokkos \cite{2022ITPDS..33..805T,2026ApJS..283...27S} across multicore processors and graphics accelerators, with particle updates taking a small fraction of the spacetime step cost at $N \le 10^4$. Two optional features of Ref.~\cite{Wu:2026nrpic} are not implemented here: the auxiliary-field shift condition and position-dependent profiles of $\eta$ and $\kappa_1$ tied to tracked black holes.
